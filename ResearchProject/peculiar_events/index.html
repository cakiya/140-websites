Peculiar Events of COVID-19

Matt Colvin, one of the two brothers from New York Times

Hoarding Business

As expected during a virus pandemic, sanitation supplies become scarce and prices go way up. Two brothers, Matt and Noah Colvin from Tennessee saw the business opportunity and drove around their city and neighboring cities to buy a small amount of hand sanitizer bottles, around 17,700. The brothers then sold them on amazon for $8-$70 dollars per bottle, around 2-10x the original price they paid for. (Vigdor 2020) They eventually got sued for price gouging (buying up supply then selling them at an outrageous price) and were forced to donate all of their stock for free to various charities and were forbidden to pull off this stunt again.

Cruise Companies’ Revenue Crash

Another side effect of the pandemic and lockdown is that vacation travel almost completely stopped as people canceled vacation plans and prepared to spend years stuck in their houses. This resulted in industries that relied on vacations to almost completely come to a halt such as cruise companies. The world’s cruise companies sat idle for an entire year straight, losing nearly 80% of their annual revenue. The only way they made it out of the pandemic was from the sheer profit they earned the years prior, enough to last them 1-2 years. The interested reader shall be directed to Denby’s video on the topic for further exploration. (Denby 2021)
