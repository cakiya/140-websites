World Impact of COVID-19

Relief Bill from Reuters

Costs

As a pandemic, COVID-19 will definitely require money spent on vaccines, and highly likely money spent on other things such as masks to prevent the spread of the virus, hospital bills to keep up with the increased number of people admitted due to the symptoms of the virus, and even paying off inflation as it rises from governments printing money to quell the pandemic. Obviously, these costs need to be paid by the governments around the world, with the U.S. expecting a 5-20% increase for their health care bill. (Abelson 2020)

Costs have also affected businesses and companies. The U.S. even passed a $2 trillion relief bill to bail out the struggling companies. (Tankersley 2020) Some of the companies straight up were not making a single dollar during the pandemic, such as cruise companies (see Peculiar Impacts LINK THIS for more info).

Inflation

The costs obviously led to massive inflation, spiking from ~2% average to nearly 5%. (Cavallo 2021) This meant that people’s money devalued twice as fast; taking only one year to devalue what would have took 2 years to devalue to. High inflation is not good for the economy as it means any money earned would devalue fast, workers more focused on spending their quickly devaluing money, and companies wouldn’t be able to fund huge projects without constant funding. Currently (in 2023) the inflation is not as high as 5%, but is still high enough that the impacts can be felt. Only time can tell whether or not the economy recovers from the COVID-19 pandemic.
